Libro 2 de The Calculus of Fortune

It is C.Y. 216 and the Gantries are an occupied district: Adjusters on every corner with folders instead of rifles, enumeration sweeps pinning fresh census plates to tenement doors, quotas raised as reprisal for one defected actuary. Anselm Serle — the Turncoat, the man who stamped a living child dead and carried the classified method across the river — now keeps the ledgers of the Long Odds, the resistance grown from salvage guilds and heretic tally-priests. His war-work is his old desk inverted: routing scheduled catastrophe back up the chain of signature, to addresses that have never known chance. As the elite experience randomness for the first time in living memory, the state reaches for its final instrument — martial law over probability itself — and the membrane holding the world's luck apart begins to tear.
