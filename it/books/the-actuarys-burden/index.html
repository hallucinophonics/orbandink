Libro 1 di The Calculus of Fortune

Probability, in this world, is a conserved quantity. Three centuries ago a census statistician proved that a prediction formally made is a constraint physically imposed — and her successors industrialised the theorem. In the Deterministic Zones, crops cannot fail and trams cannot be late, because every guaranteed outcome is paid for by an equivalent misfortune scheduled into the Variables: administered slums where buildings fall on rota and freak accidents arrive on time, because they are the timetable. Anselm Serle is the state's finest actuary. He computed one slum child's death three times — and three times filed her impossible survival. Looking for the error in the file, he finds the truth about the ledger, the child, and every certainty he has ever signed. Kafka by way of actuarial tables: the first book of the Calculus.
