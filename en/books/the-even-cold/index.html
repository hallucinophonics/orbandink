Book 3 of The Calorate

Veya Marn crosses a quarantine three thousand years old with her brother's body in the hold — wired in, burned out, freed too late. She is the wrongest Candle ever born: the one interface the network can reach into but can never wholly eat. She expects a ruin at the end of the crossing. A machine. An answer. What waits under the canopy is warm as blood, and every plant in her cargo hold has turned to face it. The jungle is not the network's homeworld. The jungle is the network's mind — grown to circulate warmth between the stars, and lobotomised by the first Stokers to make it tappable. An empire was founded on that cut. It has been starving ever since, and starvation at interstellar range wears the face of malice. Off-world, the rim is going dark in the exact order an old triage ledger predicted. Three fleets are coming with three answers, each sincere, each catastrophic. Quench a god, and hand a thousand worlds their honest weather — and a generations-long winter. Feed it, and make sacrifice permanent. Or reconnect what was cut, and stand between the stars as the one hand on every world's weather. Which is a god. However kindly meant.
