Book 6 of The Seventh Watch

Deceleration ends. The projected sky — faithful for twenty-eight thousand years — opens, and six peoples watch the stars move for the first time in living memory: a barrow-warden, a child on a storm-mast, a dawn-runner, a vine-dresser, a Vesper at a deathbed, an archivist. The last war runs through the frost ring to the helm country, with six keys gathered across five books and one page still redacted — and it opens here, where every motif of the sequence pays: the water law, the choir's song, the Chronicle, the early dusk itself. The Seventh Watch ends as elegy and dawn at once: the story of a voyage that lasted so long it became a world, arriving at the shore its passengers stopped believing in.
