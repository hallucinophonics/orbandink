Book 3 of The Breathing Dark

The black hole was never a grave. It was the mouth of a duct — a pore in skin, and the skin belongs to something alive. The Sounding spends forty days in the throat of it: a tenth of a light-year of living dark, tissue-vaults the size of moons, lumen-rivers with peristalsis in them, organs misread and then read by instruments that never lie. Iva Lenart rides it following her husband's body down — Amadou went into this dark ahead of the ship. What waits outside is the trilogy's final scale: the exhaled last of humanity standing on the outward surface of the creature that was their cosmos, in sight of a newborn universe drawing its first breath, deciding what the living owe the dying — and what anyone owes the unborn.
